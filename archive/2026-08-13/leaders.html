1. 黎巴嫩议会投票废除死刑

BBC · Lebanon parliament votes to abolish death penalty

Lebanon's parliament has voted to abolish the death penalty, a move that would make it the first country in the Middle East to formally end capital punishment.

A majority of MPs in the 128-seat parliament backed the measure on Tuesday, while Hezbollah's parliamentary bloc voted against it.

The legislation will now go to the cabinet before being passed to President Joseph Aoun for signing. The government has already expressed its support for the measure.

Lebanon has not carried out an execution since January 2004, although courts have continued to hand down death sentences. Rights groups estimate that more than 78 people remain on death row.

Under the new legislation, capital punishment will be replaced with life imprisonment with hard labour. However, the legislation does not yet set out in detail how the new sentence would be enforced.

Hard labour is already written into Lebanese law but has rarely been implemented in recent years. The country's financial crisis and overcrowded prisons have limited the authorities' resources and capacity.

The latest legislation follows years of campaigning by civil society groups and lawyers for the removal of capital punishment from Lebanese law.

"We cannot solve a crime with a crime or end violence with violence," Oragit Younan told the BBC.

As co-founder of the Lebanese Association for Civil Rights, she led a nearly three-decade campaign to abolish the death penalty in Lebanon.

"We are in a war, and people [have been] killed every day, so even in this situation, we can fight, we can struggle for human rights, and we can win. We can be a model for others," she said.

Tuesday's vote in parliament follows more than two decades of a de facto moratorium on executions. An execution required a decree signed by the president, prime minister and justice minister - but no such decree has been signed since 2004.

Under Lebanese law, the death penalty could previously be imposed for some of the country's most serious offences, including aggravated murder, certain terrorism-related crimes resulting in death, treason, espionage and offences against state security.

2. 被欧尔班罢免的前法官当选匈牙利总统

BBC · Former judge removed by Orbán elected Hungarian president

Hungary's parliament has elected András Baka, 73, a respected judge and former head of the Supreme Court, as head of state.

The Hungarian president is a largely figurehead role, but Baka's rule promises to be tempestuous.

His task will be to monitor Prime Minister Péter Magyar's efforts to dismantle the interlocking economic and political powerhouse established by former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán. He will also oversee the writing of a new constitution, to replace Orbán's constitution of 2011.

"We cannot build the new Hungary on revenge," András Baka told Parliament, after he was elected by 140 votes to 6.

"The majority of society now feels a sense of liberation and hope. Others feel disappointment and uncertainty," he said. "In a democratic society, this is not an anomaly, but a natural state of affairs."

All 51 MPs belonging to Orbán's party Fidesz walked out of the chamber before the vote, in protest against the ousting of their favourite, Tamás Sulyok, from the post last month.

In his speech, András Baka promised the broadest possible debate on the creation of a new constitution.

Since taking office in early May, the new Magyar government has lost no time attacking Orbán's legacy.

Twenty-seven laws have been passed, and two amendments to the constitution.

The states of emergency - migrants, Covid, and war in Ukraine - which Orbán used to govern by decree, were all cancelled.

The Sovereignty Protection Office, which the Fidesz government used to attack critical journalists and NGOs, was abolished, and an National Asset Recovery and Protection Office was established, with sweeping powers to investigate and prosecute individuals accused of stealing state funds under the former government.

Fidesz accuses the Tisza government of establishing a "tyranny". Ironically, they removed Baka from his post as president of the Supreme Court in 2011, for attempting to stop them undermine the rule of law.

3. 泰国大规模枪击案后暂停发放新枪支许可证

BBC · Thailand suspends new gun permits after mass shooting
💡 如果你留意东南亚控枪动态，这次政策转向可能是泰国枪支管理收紧的前奏
🔗 原文链接 📦 免费快照

Thailand's government will immediately stop issuing gun permits after a shooting incident last week in which a 14-year-old killed eight people and himself.

Existing gun owners are already banned from carrying their weapons in public.

The South East Asian country has one of the highest rates of civilian gun ownership in the region, with around 10.3 million firearms in civilian possession, and high levels of gun crime.

In response to the recent shooting, the prime minister has promised to pass tough new gun control laws with severe penalties, suspending new permits in the meantime.

Last Friday, a pupil at Debsirin Nonthaburi School outside Bangkok shot his grandparents using a handgun owned by his grandfather, before opening fire at teachers and students at school, authorities say.

Both grandparents, five staff members at the school and one 12-year-old girl died.

Police believe the shooter may have taught himself to use a gun via social media.

They say there is evidence that the boy watched violent content online, and that he showed a strong interest in learning how to handle guns.

His consumption of this content as well as anxiety over studies and family issues might have also fuelled his actions, they add.

Image source, AFPImage caption, Officials paid their respects to the victims on Tuesday at a Nonthaburi temple

Until further notice, no new permits to purchase or own guns will be issued. Existing purchase permits will not be renewed but it is unclear what will happen to existing gun ownership permits.

Permits to carry guns were suspended in late 2023 - owners must keep their weapons at home or at a shooting range.

4. 揭秘特勤局如何在众目睽睽之下隐藏美国总统的“障眼法”

BBC · Inside the Secret Service 'shell game' to hide US presidents in plain sight

🔗 原文链接 📦 免费快照

On 8 July, US President Donald Trump boarded Air Force One in plain sight of television cameras as he departed the Nato summit in Turkey.

But when the mighty Boeing aircraft left Ankara, the president had reportedly already been secretly spirited away via a catering truck to a C-32A, a military version of the Boeing 757, which flew the president to RAF Mildenhall in the UK.

Those left on board were unwitting decoys and had no indication that Trump was no longer travelling with them.

"That's very common," said Ronald Kessler, an author who has written extensively on the agency. "There's a long history of strategies to mislead anybody who might be trying to harm the president."

Robert McDonald, a former high-ranking Secret Service agent with experience working on presidential security details, told the BBC that smaller-scale deceptions are used frequently during motorcade operations.

These motorcades - which often include dozens of security and support vehicles, as well as ambulances and vans carrying White House reporters - typically include two "Beasts", as the enhanced limousine carrying the president is known.

While one vehicle carries the president, the other travels nearby as a decoy, switching locations while en route to the destination.

"It's what we call a shell game," said McDonald, who drove one of the vehicles during President George W Bush's administration. "Even in DC if we headed to Andrews [Joint Base Andrews - where Air Force One flies from], those two limousines would change position, like under a highway overpass."

"But that would happen out of sight of anybody other than those in the motorcade or in that tunnel," he added. "Different things are used to mix up the bad guy."

Image source, Getty ImagesImage caption, The order of vehicles travelling in the US president's massive motorcade is often switched en route

Similarly, Marine One - the president's helicopter - routinely flies in groups of three, changing positions so that potential threats are not sure which is carrying the US leader.
